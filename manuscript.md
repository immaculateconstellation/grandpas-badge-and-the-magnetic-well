**A story about the light that holds us**
**By Gregory Scott Davis**

*Written from the author's faith as a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.*

## Spread 1 — Grandpa's gift

When Gregory was a little boy, his grandpa made him something special.

A copper star, shining like a penny in the sun, set inside a ring of steel.

"This is your badge," Grandpa said. "Keep it close. It will remind you that you are loved."

**Art note:** Grandpa's hands giving the small badge to a little boy, warm kitchen light, the copper star catching the sun.

---

## Spread 2 — The badge

Gregory kept the badge in his pocket.

The star was copper, warm and bright. The ring was steel, strong and cool.

And the ring had a secret: it loved magnets. If you held a magnet near it, the ring would lean in — click! — and hold on tight.

**Art note:** (text page) A small copper star motif at the corner. No full illustration.

---

## Spread 3 — Building the well

One day, Gregory built a well for his badge.

First, a magnet, square and strong. Then a plate of iron. Then soft clay, patted all around. Then a cross of bright aluminum. Then a steel star with five sharp points.

And on the very top — his badge.

**Art note:** A child's hands building the stack layer by layer — magnet, iron plate, clay, aluminum cross, steel star — bright, clear, each layer easy to see.

---

## Spread 4 — The well holds

The badge sat right in the middle, snug and still.

The magnet's invisible field made a little valley — a well — and the badge rested in it.

"Magnetic materials stand in the well," Gregory said.

That was the rule of the well: the field holds what belongs in it, right in the center, safe and still.

**Art note:** (text page) A small five-pointed star motif at the corner. No full illustration.

---

## Spread 5 — The light that holds everything

Grandpa smiled. "You know what else holds things?" he said.

"God's light. God is light, and light is everywhere. It holds the stars up. It holds the earth. It holds you and me. We all live inside it, the way your badge lives inside the well."

**Art note:** The badge in the well glowing with soft golden light, stars above, the little boy looking up in wonder.

---

## Spread 6 — Right in the middle

Gregory looked at his badge, shining in the well. The star. The ring. The light.

Everything held. Everything loved.

And whenever he felt wobbly or far away, he remembered: there is a well that holds, and a light that holds, and he was right in the middle of both.

Magnetic materials stand in the well.

The end.

## For grown-ups

**The true story.** When Gregory Scott Davis was a child, his grandfather made him a badge: a copper five-pointed star set in a stainless steel ring. Years later, Gregory designed the magnetic well around it — a square neodymium magnet, a cast-iron yoke, dried clay, an aluminum cross, and an etched steel throwing star, with his grandfather's badge seated on top. A simulation of the design (2026) found the well is real: the shaped field dips in the middle and centers the badge, and the steel rim holds it fast. The badge is held, not lifted — the well centers it; it does not levitate it. The throwing star itself was checked and is magnetic. The science in this story is simplified for young children, but it is honest: fields hold, and what belongs in the well rests in the middle.

**The faith in the story.** "God is light. God is energy. We are all energy. God is the medium. We all exist in the medium." Those are the author's own words, and this book is written from his faith as a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints — the light that holds everything, the way the well holds the badge.
